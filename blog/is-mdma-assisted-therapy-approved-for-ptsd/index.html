Is MDMA-Assisted Therapy Approved for PTSD?

October 19, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: No. As of October 2026, the FDA has not approved MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD or for any other condition. The FDA declined the first application in August 2024 and asked for another trial. The sponsor, now called Resilient Pharmaceuticals, refiled in August 2026 without a new phase 3 study, and no FDA decision has been made public.

Where does FDA approval of MDMA-assisted therapy stand?

MDMA (3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine, also called midomafetamine in the drug application) remains unapproved in the United States. The National Institute on Drug Abuse states plainly that the FDA "has not approved MDMA as a treatment for any medical condition."

Here is the US timeline in brief:

  • June 4, 2024: an FDA advisory committee reviewed the application from Lykos Therapeutics. It voted 9 to 2 that the data did not show the drug works for PTSD, and 10 to 1 that the benefits did not outweigh the risks, according to the FDA summary minutes.
  • August 2024: the FDA issued a complete response letter dated August 8. That letter means the agency could not approve the drug as submitted.
  • August 2026: the company, renamed Resilient Pharmaceuticals, resubmitted its new drug application, as reported by MAPS, the nonprofit that started the program.

Advisory committee votes are not binding. The FDA makes the final call, and in this case it agreed with the panel.

What did the phase 3 trials find?

Two phase 3 trials, known as MAPP1 and MAPP2, formed the core of the application. In both, every participant got the same talk therapy. The only difference was whether the three dosing sessions used MDMA or a placebo.

MAPP1 enrolled 90 adults with severe PTSD and was published in Nature Medicine in 2021. Among people who finished treatment, scores on the Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale (CAPS-5) fell by 24.4 points with MDMA versus 13.9 points with placebo. The full paper reports that 67% of the MDMA group no longer met PTSD criteria at 18 weeks, compared with 32% on placebo.

MAPP2 enrolled 104 adults with moderate to severe PTSD and was published in Nature Medicine in 2023. CAPS-5 scores fell 23.7 points with MDMA versus 14.8 with placebo. In the full paper, 71.2% of the MDMA group no longer met PTSD criteria by study end, compared with 47.6% on placebo. Remission rates were 46.2% and 21.4%.

These are large effects on paper. Note that the placebo groups also improved a lot, because they received many hours of structured therapy. Severe adverse events occurred in 9.4% of the MDMA group and 3.9% of the placebo group in MAPP2. Neither trial reported deaths.

Why did the FDA turn it down in 2024?

The complete response letter named three main problems.

  1. Unreliable safety data. FDA inspectors found that study sites were trained not to record "positive or favorable" effects as adverse events. The FDA said this meant the trials "failed to adequately characterize" the drug's acute effects and abuse potential.
  2. No proof the benefit lasts. The trials ended 8 weeks after the last dose. The follow-up study had a single visit, timed anywhere from 6 months to 2 years later. Many participants had tried other treatments in between.
  3. Possible bias. About 40% of participants had used MDMA before. The FDA said this was far above background rates and could let people guess which group they were in.

The FDA recommended a new randomized, double-blind trial with blinded long-term follow-up. It also suggested excluding most people with prior psychedelic use and capturing every abuse-related side effect.

A separate blow landed at the same time. In August 2024 the journal Psychopharmacology retracted three papers from the MAPS research program. The editors cited "protocol violations amounting to unethical conduct" at one study site, which the authors knew about but did not disclose. These were earlier phase 2 analyses, not the MAPP1 or MAPP2 papers themselves.

What has happened since, and is it legal anywhere?

Lykos was renamed Resilient Pharmaceuticals. In August 2026, trade outlet Psychedelic Alpha reported that the company had quietly refiled its application. MAPS confirmed the application was "being submitted without a new Phase 3 study."

According to that reporting, the resubmission instead draws on an audit of the phase 3 data, outside long-term follow-up data, and a new phase 1 study of 32 healthy volunteers that tracked side effects and heart rhythm. Resilient has not discussed the filing publicly. No FDA review deadline or decision has been announced.

So the honest answer today is "not approved, and under review again." Whether the FDA accepts this evidence in place of the trial it asked for is unknown.

Outside the US, Australia is the main exception. From 1 July 2023, Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration rescheduled MDMA so that authorised psychiatrists can prescribe it for PTSD, as described in a 2024 review in the British Journal of Pharmacology. Prescribing is limited to psychiatrists approved under the TGA's Authorised Prescriber Scheme, and MDMA may be used only for PTSD.

This is not the same as a full product approval. The TGA did not evaluate and register a specific MDMA medicine. Access is narrow and runs only through specialists.

In the United States, MDMA is available only through clinical trials. You can search for active studies at ClinicalTrials.gov.

What is approved for PTSD right now?

Only two medications carry FDA approval for PTSD, both SSRI antidepressants:

  • Sertraline (Zoloft): the FDA label reports efficacy in two placebo-controlled studies of adult outpatients with PTSD.
  • Paroxetine (Paxil): the FDA label lists PTSD among its six approved adult uses.

Why is using MDMA on your own risky?

Trial MDMA was pure, measured, and given under medical watch with therapists present for hours. Street ecstasy or molly is none of those things.

  • Unknown contents. NIDA reports that drugs sold as MDMA may contain ketamine, amphetamine, synthetic cathinones, methamphetamine, or MDA. Illegally made fentanyl has also turned up in the drug supply.
  • Overheating. NIDA warns of "a dangerously steep rise in body temperature," especially with physical activity or in warm settings.
  • Mental health effects. MDMA use has been linked to anxiety and panic attacks. Regular users may have poor sleep, depression, paranoia, and memory problems.
  • No medical oversight. Trial participants tapered off psychiatric medications first, under supervision, and were monitored through each session. Taking MDMA alone removes those safeguards.

Without a therapist, MDMA can also bring up trauma memories with no support to process them. The trial protocol paired each dose with preparation and integration sessions for a reason.

The bottom line

MDMA-assisted therapy is not FDA-approved for PTSD. The FDA rejected it in August 2024 over unreliable safety data, unproven durability, and possible bias. A resubmission went in during August 2026 without the new trial the FDA requested, and a decision is pending. Australia allows authorised psychiatrists to prescribe it, but that is a narrow pathway. For now, sertraline, paroxetine, and trauma-focused therapy remain the evidence-based options in the US.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get MDMA therapy legally in the United States?

Only by enrolling in an approved clinical trial. MDMA has no FDA-approved medical use, so no US doctor can prescribe it. ClinicalTrials.gov lists studies that are recruiting.

What did the FDA advisory committee vote in 2024?

On June 4, 2024, the panel voted 9 to 2 that the data did not show MDMA was effective for PTSD. It voted 10 to 1 that the benefits did not outweigh the risks, even with the FDA's proposed safety program. The votes were advisory, but the FDA reached the same conclusion.

Who is Resilient Pharmaceuticals?

It is the new name of Lykos Therapeutics, which began as the drug-development arm of the nonprofit MAPS. It holds the MDMA application for PTSD. In August 2026 it resubmitted that application to the FDA.

Were the MAPP1 and MAPP2 trials retracted?

No. The three papers retracted in 2024 were earlier analyses of phase 2 data, published in Psychopharmacology in 2019 to 2021. The editors cited unethical conduct at one study site that the authors did not disclose. The two phase 3 papers in Nature Medicine were not retracted.

How many MDMA sessions did trial participants receive?

Three dosing sessions over about 18 weeks. Each was paired with preparation and integration therapy sessions. Everyone in the placebo group received the same therapy without MDMA.
